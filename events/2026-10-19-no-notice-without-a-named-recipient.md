title: No notice without a named recipient
---

A warning cannot leave our systems unless it names someone who can act on it.

That sounds like it was already true. It was not. The system could produce a correct, well-formed warning about a real problem and send it to nobody — counted as delivered, read by no one.

That is worse than silence, because it satisfies the part of the process meant to guarantee somebody was told.

Every exception now has an owner before it can be raised. An alert nobody owns is not an alert.
