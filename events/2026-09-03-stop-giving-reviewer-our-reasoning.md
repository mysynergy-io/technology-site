title: We stopped giving the reviewer our reasoning
---

There is a subtle way to weaken an independent review: explain too much.

If the builder sends the object together with a long account of why it should be correct, what was difficult, what was already checked and where the likely risks are, the reviewer starts from the builder's map. Even when the reviewer is independent, the search space is not.

We changed that. The reviewer gets the object, the evidence and the mandate. It does not get the builder's private reasoning about where to look.

That makes reviews less comfortable. It also makes them more useful. A reviewer that finds only the things the builder already worried about is a second reader. A reviewer that can find a failure the builder did not imagine is an independent control.
